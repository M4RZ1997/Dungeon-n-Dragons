\section*{\inTextCircled[10]{13}{15} Core Room}\stepcounter{section}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Core Room}
{\entryfont Deeper within the citadel, the remaining traces of power converge upon a single chamber. Unlike the workshops and military rooms outside, this place feels almost ceremonial - a fusion of laboratory, archive, and sanctuary built around whatever rests at its centre.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	Concentric rings of carved stone descend toward a deep well at its centre, surrounded by silent machinery and rows of weathered runes. Above the opening floats a small brass sphere, suspended within a thin stream of pale arcane energy rising from below.

	Its surface is unfinished. Several plates remain open, delicate mechanisms exposed beneath them, while empty fittings suggest that parts of the device were never completed.

	Yet even after centuries, the core still pulses faintly with power.

	As you approach, a voice suddenly speaks - not from the chamber, but from somewhere within your own thoughts.

	"Who are you? How did you find this place? And why have you come for my core? Answer me!"
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\noindent\entryfont The device suspended above the well is an unfinished energy core, one of the experimental power sources developed within Aberdour. Most of the surrounding equipment has long since failed, but a small amount of energy continues to sustain both the core and whatever presence remains connected to it.}
\subsection*{King of Aberdour}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{King of Aberdour}
{\entryfont The voice belongs to the last King of Aberdour, whose spirit has endured within the failing systems surrounding the unfinished core. Centuries of isolation have left him fiercely protective of what remains of his kingdom, and the sudden appearance of strangers within the Core Room is enough for him to assume the worst.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	"Words."

	A deep metallic groan echoes through the chamber.

	"After all this time, strangers enter my kingdom and offer me words."

	Across the chamber, an enormous construct slowly raises its head as pale light burns within its eyes. Two smaller stone guardians stir beside it.

	"Show me what stands behind them."
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\noindent\entryfont The King takes control of the dormant \hyperref[monster:Fellforged]{\LinkFont{Fellforged}}, while two \hyperref[monster:ClockworkDefender]{\LinkFont{Clockwork Defnders}} activate alongside it.\\\textbf{ROLL INITIATIVE!}}
\subsubsection*{Earning the King's Respect}
{\entryfont Although combat begins immediately, the King continues speaking through the Fellforged and can be reasoned with during the battle. He is not interested in pleading or empty promises; demonstrations of strength, resolve, and purpose make him increasingly willing to listen.

A character may use a Bonus Action on its turn to address the King and attempt a \textbf{Charisma (Persuasion) Check}. The initial DC is 18. Damaging the Fellforged proves that the expedition is more than a collection of scavengers. As the battle progresses, reduce the DC:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Fellforged below 75\% Hit Points: DC 16
	\item Fellforged below 50\% Hit Points: DC 13
	\item Fellforged below 25\% Hit Points: DC 9
\end{itemize}
Particularly meaningful arguments may grant advantage. Examples include invoking the Dwarven interpretation of the relief in the Great Plaza, explaining why the constructs of Auchtertool willingly fight for Fife, describing the threat posed by Zargothrax, or demonstrating an intent to preserve rather than exploit Aberdour's legacy.

The characters require \textbf{two successful Persuasion Checks} to convince the King. A character should accompany each check with an actual argument; repeatedly making the same appeal provides no further opportunity to persuade him.}
\subsubsection*{Defying the King}
{\entryfont With the destruction of the vessel, other consciousnesses that had remained suppressed within it are revealed: the spirits of the Gnomish engineers who once worked alongside Aberdour's Dwarves.

Freed from the King's influence, they use their remaining knowledge to stabilize the unfinished device, completing it as the \textbf{Knowledge Energy Core}.}
\subsubsection*{The King's Trial}
{\entryfont If the characters earn two successes before destroying the Fellforged, the King abruptly halts the battle. The Fellforged lowers its weapons, and the Clockwork Stone Defenders return to their dormant positions.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	“Enough.”

	The enormous construct freezes.

	For several moments, only the fading hum of its machinery fills the chamber.

	"You possess strength. More importantly, you possess the will to decide what that strength is for."

	Somewhere deeper within the chamber, ancient locks begin to disengage.

	"But spirit is not proven by words and steel alone."
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\noindent\entryfont The King reveals one final test. An ancient \hyperref[monster:WarforgedTitan]{\LinkFont{Warforged Titan}}, preserved within the Core Room for centuries, awakens to challenge the expedition.

Defeating it proves the party worthy in the King's eyes. He willingly relinquishes himself to the unfinished core, allowing his spirit and determination to become the foundation upon which it completes itself.

The expedition receives the \textbf{Spirit Energy Core}.}
