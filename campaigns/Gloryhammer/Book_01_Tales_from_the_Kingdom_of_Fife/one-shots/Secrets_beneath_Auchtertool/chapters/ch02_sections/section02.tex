\section*{\inTextCircled[10]{3}{15} Control Point}\stepcounter{section}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Control Point}
{\entryfont Farther along the transport route, the expedition reaches an old inspection station built across the tunnel. Most of its machinery has long since gone dark, but two ancient checkpoint constructs still remain at their posts. Their power reserves are nearly exhausted, yet both continue to perform duties that have been irrelevant for centuries.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	As you approach, two figures slowly stir behind the abandoned checkpoint.

	One straightens with a grinding of old joints while a dim amber light flickers behind its eyes. The other lifts its head from an ancient ledger, stares at you for several seconds, and quietly nudges its companion.

	"Travellers."

	"I see them."

	"Actual travellers."

	Another long pause follows.

	"Credentials, Cargo Declaration and destination, please."
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\noindent\entryfont The constructs are cautious but far more curious than hostile. They have not encountered anyone new in centuries and quickly become fascinated by the expedition, particularly the unfamiliar construction of its members. Their original protocols require them to inspect travellers and cargo, but neither remembers those procedures particularly well.

Both are operating on extremely low power. Sentences occasionally trail off while systems temporarily shut down, movements occur several seconds after decisions are made, and arguments may be interrupted by long periods of complete silence. The larger Warden tries to remain professional and procedural, while the smaller Clerk is openly curious and frequently wastes precious power asking unnecessary questions.

The characters do not possess valid ancient credentials, but almost any plausible explanation, official document, clever argument, or sufficiently confident improvisation can eventually convince the pair to let them pass. Combat should only occur if the expedition deliberately attacks them.}

\subsection*{A Matter of Time}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{A Matter of Time}
{\entryfont What the characters overhear upon approaching depends on the Clearance Time recorded at the Collapsed Tunnel. If Silas reaches the checkpoint before the rest of the expedition, use Rapid Clearance.}

\subsubsection*{Rapid Clearance - The Old Crane}
{\entryfont The constructs are discussing an ancient irregularity that apparently happened so long ago neither can remember when. Someone repeatedly lingered around one of the nearby loading cranes without ever taking anything.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	"Nobody visits the same crane that often without a reason."

	"Perhaps they liked the crane."

	"Nobody likes cranes that much."
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\noindent\entryfont Questioning them reveals little more, but the story provides a clue toward the Hidden Cache.}
\subsubsection*{Delayed Clearance - Strange Noises}
{\entryfont The constructs recently detected an unfamiliar sound farther along the transport route and are debating who should investigate.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	"You should go."

	"Power reserve: three percent."

	"Mine is two."

	A long silence follows.

	"We should wait."

	"Agreed."
\end{DndReadAloud}
\subsubsection*{Prolonged Clearance - Low Power}
{\entryfont By the time the expedition arrives, both constructs have entered a reduced-power state. They still perform the basic inspection, but their responses are slow and minimal, and the expedition learns no additional information.}
