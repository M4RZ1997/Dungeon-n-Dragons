\clearpage\section*{The Hunters}\stepcounter{section}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The Hunters}
{\entryfont On their return journey to Ty Coeden, the party decides to take the longer path along the western shores of Loch Leven. The route has traditionally been used by Caelthir scouts to watch for Fey creatures emerging from nearby portals, though no such reports have been made since the ritual.

As evening approaches, raised voices echo through the trees.

A small group of local hunters is gathered around the carcass of a stag.

Unlike the lumberjacks, these hunters have little interest in diplomacy. They are concerned with a problem that has appeared only within the last day.}

\subsection*{Foul Game}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Foul Game}
{\entryfont The hunting party consists of four experienced locals. They are wary of the strangers, but far more worried about what they have discovered.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	Their leader explains:

	\textit{"This is the third one today."}
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\entryfont When asked what happened, they admit they have no idea.

Every animal they've found today has died in exactly the same way.}

\subsection*{The Carcass}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{The Carcass}
{\entryfont The stag appears almost untouched.

At first glance, it seems as though it simply collapsed.

Closer examination reveals several unsettling details:
\begin{itemize}
	\item No flesh has been eaten.
	\item No obvious wounds are visible.
	\item The body is unusually cold despite having died recently.
	\item The surrounding vegetation is undisturbed.
	\item Birds and scavengers refuse to approach.
\end{itemize}
A successful Medicine or Nature check reveals that the animal did not die from disease or poison. Something killed it suddenly, but left almost no evidence behind.}

\subsection*{Following the Trail}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Following the Trail}
{\entryfont The hunters ask whether the party would be willing to help investigate before darkness falls.

Searching the surrounding forest uncovers a trail that seems strangely inconsistent.

Possible discoveries include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item unusually deep paw prints that resemble those of a fox, yet are far too large
	\item broken saplings at shoulder height
	\item tufts of cobalt-blue fur caught on brambles
	\item trees bearing long claw marks several feet above the ground
	\item fleeting glimpses of movement that vanish before anyone gets a clear look
\end{itemize}
The tracks weave unpredictably through the woods, as though the creature paid little attention to natural obstacles.}

\subsection*{An Unnatural Silence}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{An Unnatural Silence}
{\entryfont As the party follows the trail deeper into the forest, they begin to notice the woodland itself.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	The usual sounds gradually disappear.
	
	No birds sing.
	
	No insects buzz.
	
	Even the wind seems strangely absent beneath the canopy.
	
	The hunters eventually refuse to continue.
	
	One quietly admits:
	
	"I've hunted these woods for thirty years."
	
	"I've never heard them this quiet."
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\entryfont Whether the players press onward or not, the silence itself should become the strongest warning.}

\subsection*{The Trail Ends}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{The Trail Ends}
{\entryfont Eventually the tracks lead into a rocky thicket overlooking Loch Leven.

There, the party finds only signs that something rested here briefly:
\begin{itemize}
	\item flattened undergrowth
	\item several broken stones
	\item deep claw impressions in exposed rock
	\item fresh fox prints leading away in three different directions
\end{itemize}
There is no creature to fight.

Whatever left the trail is already gone.}
