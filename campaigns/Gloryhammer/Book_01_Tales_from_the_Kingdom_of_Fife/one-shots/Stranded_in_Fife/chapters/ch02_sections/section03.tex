\section*{The Hunters}\stepcounter{section}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The Hunters}
{\entryfont While travelling through the Woods of Lomond near the western shores of Loch Leven, the characters hear raised voices carrying between the trees. Ahead, four local hunters stand around the carcass of a stag.

The route is commonly used by Caelthir scouts watching the surrounding woods and the shores of the loch, but the hunters themselves are ordinary people of Fife.}
\subsection*{Foul Game}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Foul Game}
{\entryfont The hunting party consists of four experienced locals. They are wary of the unfamiliar travellers, but their concern over what they have found quickly outweighs their suspicion.
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	The eldest hunter looks up as you approach, one hand still resting on the strap of his bow.

	His gaze briefly passes over the group before returning to the stag at his feet.

	"Third one in as many days."

	He nudges the carcass lightly with his boot.

	"And we still don't know what's killing them."
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\noindent\entryfont The hunters discovered all three animals within the last few days. None showed signs of having been hunted for food, and each appeared to have died suddenly. If the characters show an interest in helping, the hunters readily allow them to examine the latest carcass.}
\subsubsection*{The Carcass}
{\entryfont At first glance, the stag appears strangely untouched. It lies on its side among the grass as though it simply collapsed where it stood. The characters may examine the carcass more closely and may reveal different pieces of information.
\paragraph*{Wisdom (Medicine) Check (DC 12)} The stag died suddenly and only a few hours ago. It shows no signs of disease, poisoning, or prolonged blood loss. Several small injuries were inflicted shortly before death, but none should have been immediately fatal.
\paragraph*{Intelligence (Nature) Check (DC 12)} The wounds are consistent with claws and teeth, yet the carcass bears none of the feeding marks expected from an ordinary predator. Even scavengers appear unusually reluctant to approach it.

No single check identifies the killer. Taken together, however, the evidence makes one thing clear: this was not the work of an ordinary predator.}
\subsection*{Following the Trail}\stepcounter{subsection}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Following the Trail}
{\entryfont If the characters offer to help, the hunters lead them to the place where the stag was first discovered. From there, faint signs of passage continue deeper into the woods.

The trail is fragmented and strangely inconsistent, but enough traces remain for the characters to follow it.
\paragraph*{Wisdom (Survival) Check (DC 12)} The tracks do not form a normal pursuit. They cross one another repeatedly, change direction without warning, and occasionally disappear for several yards before resuming elsewhere.
\paragraph*{Wisdom (Perception) Check (DC 13)} Several trees bear fresh scratches well above the ground, while nearby stones show narrow claw marks. Similar traces appear on opposite sides of the trail.}
\subsubsection*{An Unnatural Silence}
{\entryfont As the characters follow the trail deeper into the woods, the surrounding forest gradually changes. The tracks remain visible, but the ordinary sounds of the woodland begin to disappear one by one.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	At first, it is difficult to notice exactly what has changed.

	Then the absence becomes impossible to ignore.

	No birds sing above you. No insects stir beneath the leaves. Even the wind seems muted beneath the canopy.
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\noindent\entryfont The hunters are visibly unsettled and become increasingly reluctant to continue. They do not know what caused the silence, but instinct tells them that whatever lies ahead is not something they wish to confront.}
\subsubsection*{The Trail Ends}
{\entryfont The trail eventually leads into a rocky thicket overlooking Loch Leven.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	Several rocks have been cracked or overturned, and deep scratches score their surfaces. The surrounding brush has been pressed down in several places, as though something large rested here.

	Then the trail simply stops.
\end{DndReadAloud}
