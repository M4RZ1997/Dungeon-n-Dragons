Within their hidden village of Ty Coeden, Fey Eladrin, Korreds, Satyrs, Centaurs, and other Fey creatures work together, combining their knowledge in hopes of one day reopening a stable path home. % ADDITIONAL FEATURES & TRAITS
\AdditionalFeaturesAndTraits{
	Ciaran treats every conversation as a performance, effortlessly weaving compliments, stories, and gentle humor into even the briefest encounter. He keeps a small journal filled with unusual customs, songs, and historical anecdotes gathered from every settlement he visits, convinced they will one day inspire the perfect tale. Since arriving in Ty Coeden, he has enthusiastically embraced Caelthir traditions, though he occasionally misinterprets their customs with amusing results. He has a habit of offering fresh flowers or handwoven wreaths as tokens of appreciation after memorable meetings. Quiet moments often find him practicing melodies inspired by birdsong and rustling leaves, constantly refining his repertoire. Although naturally sociable, he rarely reveals his own worries, preferring to maintain an air of confidence and delight. Secretly, he fears that if his charm ever fails, others may find him far less remarkable than the persona he so carefully presents.
}

% BACKGROUND
\Characterbackground{
	Ciaran Bramblehorn spent much of his life wandering the Feywild, where satyr revels, timeless feasts, and grand performances shaped his love of music and storytelling. Fascinated by the tales behind every melody, he sought forgotten ballads and ancient legends, believing that understanding another's history was the key to earning their admiration. His remarkable charm eventually drew the attention of the College of Glamour, where he refined both his artistry and his enchanting presence.

Only recently, one of the unpredictable Fey portals carried Ciaran into the Woods of Lomond. Unable to return, he was welcomed into Ty Coeden by the Caelthir Tribe, who recognized him as another soul stranded between worlds. Though grateful for their hospitality, he still views his stay as temporary and eagerly questions anyone who might know more about the unstable portals.

Ciaran has quickly become a familiar face at village celebrations, where his performances lift spirits and strengthen bonds among the refugees. Beneath his effortless charisma, however, he carefully studies those around him, believing every person carries a story worth discovering—and that winning a heart begins with truly understanding it.
}

\chapter*{Features, Magic Items and Spells}

\section*{Satyr Traits}
\subsection*{Ram}
You can use your head and horns to make unarmed strikes. When you hit with them, the strike deals 1d6 + your Strength modifier bludgeoning damage, instead of the bludgeoning damage normal for an unarmed strike.
\subsection*{Magic Resistance}
You have advantage on saving throws against spells.
\subsection*{Mirthful Leaps}
Whenever you make a long jump or a high jump, you can roll a d8 and add the number rolled to the number of feet you cover, even when making a standing jump. This extra distance costs movement as usual.
\subsection*{Reveler}
As an embodiment of revelry, you have proficiency in the Performance and Persuasion skills, and you have proficiency with one musical instrument of your choice.

\section*{Feats}
\subsection*{Tireless Reveler}
When an ally you can see within 60 feet of yourself expends Heroic Inspiration, you can gain Heroic Inspiration if you lack it. You can use this benefit a number of times equal to your Proficiency Bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Short or Long Rest.

\section*{Bard Traits}
\subsection*{Bardic Inspiration}
\textbf{Uses: \intcalcAdd{0}{\calculateModifier{\CharismaScoreValue}}, d8}\\
You can supernaturally inspire others through words, music, or dance. This inspiration is represented by your Bardic Inspiration die, which is a d6.

\paragraph*{Using Bardic Inspiration} As a Bonus Action, you can inspire another creature within 60 feet of yourself who can see or hear you. That creature gains one of your Bardic Inspiration dice. A creature can have only one Bardic Inspiration die at a time.

Once within the next hour when the creature fails a D20 Test, the creature can roll the Bardic Inspiration die and add the number rolled to the d20, potentially turning the failure into a success. A Bardic Inspiration die is expended when it's rolled.

\paragraph*{Number of Uses} You can confer a Bardic Inspiration die a number of times equal to your Charisma modifier (minimum of once), and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

\paragraph*{At Higher Levels} Your Bardic Inspiration die changes when you reach certain Bard levels, as shown in the Bardic Die column of the Bard Features table. The die becomes a \textbf{d8 at level 5}, a d10 at level 10, and a d12 at level 15.
\subsection*{Expertise}
\textbf{Performance, History Expertise}\\
You gain Expertise in two of your skill proficiencies of your choice. Performance and Persuasion are recommended if you have proficiency in them.

At Bard level 9, you gain Expertise in two more of your skill proficiencies of your choice.
\subsection*{Jack of All Trades}
You can add half your Proficiency Bonus (round down) to any ability check you make that uses a skill proficiency you lack and that doesn't otherwise use your Proficiency Bonus.

For example, if you make a Strength (Athletics) check and lack Athletics proficiency, you can add half your Proficiency Bonus to the check.
\subsection*{College of Glamour}
\subsubsection*{Mantle of Inspiration}
When you join the College of Glamour at 3rd level, you gain the ability to weave a song of fey magic that imbues your allies with vigor and speed.

As a bonus action, you can expend one use of your Bardic Inspiration to grant yourself a wondrous appearance. When you do so, choose a number of creatures you can see and that can see you within 60 feet of you, up to a number equal to your Charisma modifier (minimum of one). Each of them gains 5 temporary hit points. When a creature gains these temporary hit points, it can immediately use its reaction to move up to its speed, without provoking opportunity attacks.

The number of temporary hit points increases when you reach certain levels in this class, increasing to \textbf{8 at 5th level}, 11 at 10th level, and 14 at 15th level.
\subsubsection*{Enthralling Performance}
Starting at 3rd level, you can charge your performance with seductive, fey magic.

If you perform for at least 1 minute, you can attempt to inspire wonder in your audience by singing, reciting a poem, or dancing. At the end of the performance, choose a number of humanoids within 60 feet of you who watched and listened to all of it, up to a number equal to your Charisma modifier (minimum of one). Each target must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw against your spell save DC or be charmed by you. While charmed in this way, the target idolizes you, it speaks glowingly of you to anyone who talks to it, and it hinders anyone who opposes you, although it avoids violence unless it was already inclined to fight on your behalf. This effect ends on a target after 1 hour, if it takes any damage, if you attack it, or if it witnesses you attacking or damaging any of its allies.

If a target succeeds on its saving throw, the target has no hint that you tried to charm it.

Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a short or long rest.
\subsubsection*{Mantle of Majesty}
At 6th level, you gain the ability to cloak yourself in a fey magic that makes others want to serve you. As a bonus action, you cast command, without expending a spell slot, and you take on an appearance of unearthly beauty for 1 minute or until your concentration ends (as if you were concentrating on a spell). During this time, you can cast command as a bonus action on each of your turns, without expending a spell slot.
