\DndDropCapLine{I}{\entryfont n the heart of Fyfe lies the Woods of Lomond, a vast and brooding forest shrouded in mystery. Its dense, shadowed canopy discourages all but the most foolhardy from venturing within, and few who do ever return. Whispers tell of a hidden elven tribe dwelling deep among the trees, unseen and uninterested in the affairs of nearby villages. Stranger tales speak of curious creatures haunting the forest's edges, and of wanderers who vanish without trace - neither bodies nor belongings ever found, as if swallowed by another realm. On rare occasions, someone emerges claiming to have grown up in the Woods, yet their memories are fragmented, elusive, and often dismissed as fanciful lies.}

\section*{Ty Coeden}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Ty Coeden}
{\entryfont Hidden deep within the Woods of Lomond lies Ty Coeden, the living heart of the Caelthir Tribe. The village is less built than grown, shaped by Feywild magic into an otherworldly settlement where towering trees bend into archways, roots twist into bridges, and softly glowing flora illuminate winding paths. Structures seem to shift subtly depending on the angle of view, as if the village itself resists being fully understood by mortal perception.

Despite its beauty, Ty Coeden is tense. The Caelthir maintain constant vigilance against the unstable Feywild portals that sporadically appear throughout the forest. Watchers perch in high branches, unseen sentinels linger between veils of illusion, and faintly glowing sigils mark hidden defensive wards. The elves are wary of outsiders but not immediately hostile - years of dealing with unpredictable Fey incursions have taught them caution over aggression. When the party arrives, they are quickly assessed and deemed a low immediate threat, though never truly trusted. The village's focus remains outward, toward the woods, as if expecting something to emerge at any moment.}

\begin{DndReadAloud}
	As you pass between the ancient, close-woven trees, the forest begins to... change. The air grows softer, tinged with a faint silver-blue glow, and the rigid forms of the woods give way to something more fluid, more dreamlike. Trees curve impossibly, their trunks arching into graceful doorways, while roots coil into spiralling walkways suspended above the forest floor. Leaves shimmer faintly, catching light that seems to come from nowhere - and everywhere at once.

	You begin to notice movement. Not open, not obvious - but there. A figure high in the branches, perfectly still. A flicker between two twisting roots. The unmistakable sensation of being watched from all sides.

	The village itself feels alive, as though it breathes in quiet harmony with the forest. Yet beneath its beauty lies tension. Faint runes pulse along bark and stone, and more than once you catch the glint of drawn bows or the subtle shift of a hidden blade. These people are not at ease.

	As you step further into the settlement, a few of the unseen watchers briefly turn their attention toward you - measuring, judging - before their focus snaps back outward, toward the surrounding woods, as if you are not the greatest danger here.

	Then, from between two curved tree-arches, a small troop of elven warriors emerges, their movements silent and precise, eyes fixed firmly upon you.
\end{DndReadAloud}
\subsection*{Elder Council}
{\entryfont The Elder Council of the Caelthir convenes within a great living structure at the heart of Ty Coeden, formed from intertwined trunks and luminous branches. The chamber is open yet enclosed, its walls shifting subtly as if shaped by unseen will. Here, the eldest and most learned of the Caelthir gather to deliberate on matters of survival and the growing instability of the Feywild portals.

When the party is brought before them, the atmosphere is solemn and tense, as though every word carries weight beyond the moment.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	The elders listen in silence before one of them - ancient, silver-eyed - leans forward slightly, their voice calm but heavy with burden.

	"Know this, outsiders: the veil between worlds is broken here."

	"There is no pattern to the openings. They come and go as they please... yet we have observed a troubling truth. The closer one travels to Loch Leven, the more frequent these tears become."

	Another elder raises a slender hand, tracing a faint glowing sigil in the air.

	"The waters themselves are not safe. Portals open beneath the surface of Loch Leven."

	A pause follows, heavier this time.

	"Most who come through there... do not survive. Their bodies are cast ashore days later - broken, changed. The crossing is... unforgiving."

	Another elder's gaze hardens.

	"And yet, even in death, they are a warning. The veil is weakest there."

	A third speaks, their tone sharper.

	"And what comes through... is not singular in nature. Some are harmless. Lost. Frightened. But others..."
	They pause.
	"...others are hunters."

	"We have taken it upon ourselves to contain what we can. To shelter those who are not a threat. To destroy those who are. This burden is ours - but it does not end with us."

	A quiet murmur passes through the council before the first elder speaks again.

	"In our oldest research, fractured as they are, there is mention of a place... a structure... a castle of water. We believe it may be tied to all of this."

	A final pause.

	"Our only lead lies upon an island - St. Serf's Inch. Yet even our scouts dare not linger there."

	Silence falls over the chamber.
\end{DndReadAloud}
\subsubsection*{What the Council Knows}
{\entryfont The Elder Council can provide the following information:
\begin{itemize}\renewcommand\labelitemi{\textbf{\textbullet}}
	\item \textbf{Unstable Portals.} The appearance of Feywild portals is erratic and unpredictable, with no discernible pattern.
	\item \textbf{Loch Leven.} Portal activity increases significantly near Loch Leven, suggesting a weakened boundary in that region.
	\item \textbf{Water-borne Manifestations.} Portals can open within the waters of the loch. Most creatures emerging there do not survive, their bodies later washing ashore.
	\item \textbf{Fey Creatures.} The portals bring forth a wide range of beings, from harmless creatures to highly dangerous monstrosities.
	\item \textbf{Caelthir Role.} The Caelthir act as guardians, sheltering benign Fey creatures while eliminating hostile threats.
	\item \textbf{The Water Castle.} Fragmented records speak of a "water castle" tied to this location. The only known lead points to St. Serf's Inch in Loch Leven, though repeated expeditions have found nothing. The area remains highly unstable and difficult to investigate.
\end{itemize}}
\subsubsection*{If the players ask...}
{\entryfont\begin{itemize}\renewcommand\labelitemi{\textbf{\textbullet}}
	\item \textbf{About returning to the Feywild.} The council admits they have no reliable method, though they continue their research.
	\item \textbf{About assistance.} The Caelthir cannot spare many warriors, as they must defend the woods, but may offer limited aid or guidance.
	\item \textbf{About stopping the portals.} The council believes the source lies near Loch Leven, though its exact nature is unknown.
	\item \textbf{About joining the fight against Zargothrax.} The council does not grant their support lightly - such an alliance must be earned (see \hyperref[alliance:CaelthirElves]{\LinkFont{Alliances, Caelthir Elves}}).
\end{itemize}}

Should the party slay only the adult foxes and leave the cubs behind, an unseen chain of events is set into motion. The surviving young will not remain harmless for long.

{\entryfont If the party slays the adult Cobalt Foxes but leaves one or more cubs alive, the balance of the Feywild influence in the region is violently disrupted.

Two days after the event, the surviving cubs undergo a rapid and unnatural transformation, emerging as Zircon Foxes. Driven by instinct and vengeance, three of these creatures descend upon Ty Coeden, launching a sudden and devastating assault. The attack results in numerous casualties among the Caelthir and the destruction of large parts of the settlement's living structures.

From this point onward, the party becomes marked by the consequences of their actions:

\section*{Loch Leven}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Loch Leven}
{\entryfont Nestled in the western parts of the Woods of Lomond, Loch Leven lies still and deceptively serene, its dark waters reflecting the canopy above like a polished mirror. Yet along its muddy shores, the illusion of tranquillity is shattered. The banks are littered with the lifeless remains of Feywild creatures - elves, beasts, and stranger beings - washed ashore after emerging from unstable portals beneath the lake's surface. Some bear no wounds, their lungs filled with water, while others are marked by blade and arrow, evidence of the Caelthir's relentless efforts to contain the threats spilling into this realm. The air hangs heavy with decay and lingering magic, and the waters themselves seem restless, as if something beneath is stirring - or waiting.}
\subsection*{St. Serf's Inch}
{\entryfont St. Serf's Inch is a large, heavily forested island within Loch Leven, spanning roughly 3,000 feet in width and 1,500 feet in length. Dense woodland covers most of its surface, with thick undergrowth, tangled roots, and uneven terrain that makes travel slow and disorienting.

While bodies from the loch still wash ashore here, they are noticeably fewer in number than along the mainland shores. Those that do appear are often in a more disturbing state - particularly the remains of bullywugs are missing limbs or sections of their bodies, removed with unnaturally clean precision. These signs suggest that something on the island is interfering with the remains after they arrive.

Despite repeated assumptions, no visible structure resembling a "water castle" can be found on the island.}
\subsubsection*{The Corpseweaver}
{\entryfont Deep within the forest of St. Serf's Inch lies a crude encampment, occupied by a solitary \hyperref[monster:CentipedeHag]{\LinkFont{Centipede Hag}} who has claimed this place as her own. It is not hidden particularly well, but is positioned in an area where the forest grows unnaturally thick, with twisted roots and damp, uneven ground slowing movement and limiting visibility.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	The forest grows thicker as you press onward, the undergrowth twisting into a tangled maze of roots and damp earth. A foul stench begins to seep into the air - rot, smoke, and something far worse.

	Ahead, the trees part just enough to reveal a crude clearing. A crooked table stands at its center, strewn with dark tools and something that might once have been a body. Nearby, a heavy iron kettle hangs over a roaring fire, its contents bubbling thickly as misshapen limbs break the surface before sinking again.

	Bones and scraps of flesh litter the ground, picked clean in places, carefully cut in others.

	And somewhere within the clearing, something shifts.
\end{DndReadAloud}
\paragraph*{Encampment Features}\hfill\\
{\entryfont The hag's camp is a disturbing blend of crude survival and grotesque experimentation:
\begin{itemize}\renewcommand\labelitemi{\textbf{\textbullet}}
	\item \textbf{Makeshift Worktable.} At the center of the camp stands a rough wooden table, stained dark with old blood. Scattered across it are crude tools, bone needles, and sinew thread. Upon the table lies a partially assembled corpse - an unfinished attempt at creating a twisted servant. Limbs from different creatures have been crudely stitched together, but the result is inert and lifeless. The hag's efforts have so far failed, though it is clear she continues to experiment.
	\item \textbf{Iron Kettle.} Suspended over a large, constantly burning fire hangs a heavy iron kettle. Inside, a grisly stew slowly bubbles - recognizable limbs of bullywugs and other creatures protrude from the murky liquid. The smell is overwhelming up close, and the contents are clearly intended as both sustenance and material for further use.
	\item \textbf{Scattered Remains.} Around the camp lie discarded bones and partially butchered corpses, many of them missing limbs with unnaturally clean cuts. These match the remains found along the island's shore, confirming the source of the disturbances.
\end{itemize}}
\paragraph*{Encountering the Hag}\hfill\\
{\entryfont The Centipede Hag is typically found within her camp, either tending to her failed creations or preparing her next meal. She is aware of her surroundings and difficult to surprise unless the party takes significant precautions.
\begin{itemize}
	\item The hag is not immediately hostile.
	\item She may observe the party briefly before revealing herself, sizing them up as potential threats - or resources.
	\item If approached cautiously, she may attempt to engage in conversation, particularly if she believes she can gain something from the party.
\end{itemize}}
\paragraph*{Behaviour \& Tactics}
{\entryfont\begin{itemize}
	\item The hag prefers manipulation over immediate violence, using her unsettling demeanour and cryptic speech to unnerve intruders.
	\item If combat breaks out, she uses her mobility and magic to isolate targets and inflict debilitating effects.
	\item She will not fight to the death unless cornered, preferring to retreat into the forest if the situation turns against her.
\end{itemize}}

\subsection*{Lochleven Castle}\label{subsec:LochlevenCastle}
{\entryfont To the west of Loch Leven, a submerged ruin lies hidden beneath the lake's dark, murky waters. Visibility below the surface is extremely limited, obscuring the full extent of the structure.

Only a single feature is visible above water: a weathered stone column - the remains of a tower - protruding slightly above the surface. Its irregular shape makes it difficult to distinguish from natural rock formations, which explains why it has long gone unnoticed by the Caelthir, who are unfamiliar with material plane architecture.

The rest of the castle lies entirely submerged beneath the lake.

As the party approaches the column, an Incarnation of Transcience emerges from below and attacks, having claimed the area as its territory.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	Ahead, something breaks the surface. A jagged column of stone rises from the water, worn smooth by time and waves. At first glance, it seems little more than a natural formation - but the longer you look, the more its shape feels... deliberate. Structured.

	As you draw closer, the lake remains still. No current, no disturbance  - just that silent, watching surface.

	Then - Something moves below you.

	A shape glides through the dark water, barely visible. It passes through stone and silt as though neither existed, its form shifting, indistinct.

	No ripple. No wake. It rises.

	An unnatural presence breaches the surface, its body phasing subtly as it moves - something that does not belong to this world.

	It reacts instantly to your presence.
\end{DndReadAloud}
{\paragraph*{Encounter}\entryfont The \hyperref[monster:IncarnationOfTranscience]{\LinkFont{Incarnation of Transcience}} attacks immediately upon revealing itself, defending its territory around the ruin. It fights instinctively and does not retreat unless forced.}
\subsubsection*{\inTextCircled[10]{A}{10} Entrance}
{\entryfont At the top of the protruding stone column, a narrow opening can be found - what remains of a sealed hatch, now worn and partially exposed by time and water. The surrounding stone is slick and uneven, requiring careful footing to approach.

Opening it reveals a vertical shaft descending into darkness. A wooden ladder, aged yet remarkably well preserved, leads downward into the submerged structure below. Despite the ruin lying beneath the waters of Loch Leven, no water fills the shaft. The air rising from below is cool, stale, and dry, carrying the scent of old stone rather than the dampness of a flooded ruin.}
\subsubsection*{Exploring Lochleven Castle}
{\entryfont The interior of Lochleven Castle appears largely intact, its stonework preserved in a state that defies its submerged surroundings. Though age is evident in worn surfaces and creeping overgrowth, there are few signs of structural collapse. The absence of water within the halls and chambers creates an unsettling contrast to the reality outside, lending the castle an unnatural stillness.

The following sections detail the key locations within the castle as the party progresses toward the ritual chamber. A map of the dungeon is provided to assist in tracking movement and spatial relationships between areas.}
\subsection*{\inTextCircled[10]{1}{10} Pillar-Room}
{\entryfont The chamberopens into a broad, pillar-lined hall that immediately establishes the unnatural state of the structure.

Illumination is provided by bioluminescent flora of Feywild origin. Delicate blossoms and slender vines are carefully interwoven along the pillars and upper walls, their soft, steady glow bathing the chamber in bright, even light.

In contrast, a far more aggressive growth emerges from the lower levels of the castle. Thick, tangled vines - darker and more irregular - creep upward along the stairway and into the chamber, encroaching upon the otherwise orderly arrangement.}
\begin{DndReadAloud}
	As you descend into the castle, the air shifts - still, dry, and unnervingly calm.

	The chamber before you is far larger than expected, its high ceiling supported by thick stone pillars that stretch upward into shadow. Yet it is not darkness that fills this place.

	A soft, radiant light spills across the hall, cast not by flame, but by carefully arranged clusters of glowing plants. Their blossoms shine with steady brilliance, their light clean and even, as though placed with deliberate purpose.

	But along the stair behind you - and creeping in from below - thicker vines twist upward, darker and more tangled, pushing into the chamber where they do not belong.

	There is no sign of water. No damp stone. No decay.

	Only silence... and the quiet glow of something not meant for this world.
\end{DndReadAloud}

	\DndSetThemeColor[PhbTan]
	\begin{DndComment}{DM Annotation: Temporal Distortion}
		Lochleven Castle is affected by a localized temporal anomaly originating from the failed ritual within its depths. This distortion does not halt time entirely, but instead disrupts the natural processes that would normally affect the structure. Within the castle:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item \textbf{Flooding is prevented.} The surrounding waters of Loch Leven cannot cross the boundary of the anomaly. Despite the structure being fully submerged, the interior remains completely dry.
			\item \textbf{Decay is suspended.} Organic and inorganic materials do not deteriorate as they normally would. Wood, cloth, and other materials remain intact despite the passage of time.
			\item \textbf{Ageing is halted.} Creatures within the affected area do not age naturally. However, they still suffer from injury, exhaustion, and other effects as normal.
			\item \textbf{Time flows normally for actions.} Movement, combat, and spellcasting function without restriction. The distortion affects only long-term processes, not moment-to-moment activity.
			\item \textbf{Environmental stillness.} Air currents are minimal, sounds are slightly dampened, and small particles may linger longer than expected before settling.
		\end{itemize}
		\noindent The source of this distortion lies within the ritual chamber deeper in the castle. Until the temporal anchor is restored, these effects persist throughout the structure.
	\end{DndComment}

\subsection*{\inTextCircled[10]{2}{10} Overgrown Gallery}
{\entryfont A narrow, elevated stone walkway connects two sections of the castle, suspended above a lower chamber. Once a simple passage, it has now been almost entirely overtaken by rampant Feywild growth.

Thick vines coil around the stone railings and spill across the walkway itself, forming a dense tangle of roots, thorns, and luminous blossoms. In many places, the original stone surface is no longer visible, buried beneath layers of intertwined plant matter.

To cross the overgrowth, a character must succeed on a \textbf{DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) Check}. On a failure, the character takes \DndDice{1d6} Piercing damage and becomes Restrained (Escape DC 14). If the check fails by 5 or more, the character loses their footing and falls 10 feet into the \hyperref[sec:OvergrownLaboratory]{\LinkFont{\inTextCircled[10]{12}{10} Overgrown Laboratory}} below.

Beyond the walkway, the growth thickens even further. Forcing a path through requires a \textbf{DC 17 Strength (Athletics) Check}. On a failure by 5 or less, the character still pushes through but takes \DndDice{2d6} Piercing damage from the thorns. On a greater failure, the overgrowth proves too dense to pass.}
\subsubsection*{Harvesting Feywild Blossoms}
{\entryfont The luminous blossoms growing among the vines can be harvested with a successful \textbf{DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana or Nature) Check}. On a failure, the character takes \DndDice{1d6} Piercing damage. If the check fails by 5 or more, the character instead takes \DndDice{3d6} Piercing damage and becomes Restrained (Escape DC 14).}

\subsection*{\inTextCircled[10]{3}{10} Grandma's Quarters}
{\entryfont This small chamber stands in stark contrast to the rest of the castle. The room is warm, orderly, and well-kept, with an almost uncanny sense of normalcy.

Simple furnishings fill the space: a neatly made bed, a wooden chair, and a small table set with baking utensils and ingredients. Shelves along the walls are lined with jars of preserves, dried herbs, and neatly labeled containers. A softly glowing Feylight plant has been carefully arranged to illuminate the room, casting a gentle, homely light.

Seated at the table is an elderly woman, calmly tending to a tray of freshly baked cookies. She appears entirely at ease, as though nothing in the castle were amiss.}
\subsubsection*{Grandma Mac Braith}
{\entryfont Grandma Mac Braith is warm, hospitable, and gently talkative, welcoming the party without hesitation as though they were expected guests. She assumes they are acquaintances or colleagues of her grandson, Duncan, and treats them accordingly, offering food and a place to rest. Despite the state of the castle, she remains completely unconcerned with any danger or unusual circumstances, speaking as though everything is perfectly normal and ongoing.
