\section*{Dragon's Gold}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Dragon's Gold}
\textbf{Entry Fee:} 1 GP\\
{\entryfont The Dragon's Gold is the crown jewel of the festival, a dazzling and immersive event that draws crowds of eager participants and onlookers to the central plaza. The centrepiece of this spectacle is the magnificent dragon itself, a towering construct of painted paper, wood, and metal. Adorned with shimmering scales in vibrant reds, greens, and golds, the dragon moves with startling realism. Clever mechanisms allow its angular head to snap and jerk, its wooden wings to flutter with a show of might, and its mouth to "breathe fire" in the form of colourful ribbons and occasional bursts of magical smoke. The creature prowls menacingly near its sprawling treasure hoard, exuding an air of majesty and danger that captivates the entire square.}

\subsection*{Dragon's Gold - Mechanics}
{\entryfont The Dragon's Gold game takes place in a 50-foot radius playing area, covered in thick layers of hay to conceal scattered treasures. At the heart of the area lies the 15-foot radius \textbf{Inner Hoard}, where the most coveted prizes are clustered. However, this inner zone is guarded by the \hyperref[monster:FestivalDragon]{\LinkFont{Festival Dragon}}, which relentlessly defends its treasures.}

{\entryfont\noindent Participants enter the game unarmed and unarmoured, as no equipment is allowed. The use of magic is strictly prohibited, and contestants may not harm or attack one another under any circumstances, preserving the festive nature of the game.

Each round lasts 1 minute (10 rounds in total). Contestants have this time to search for and claim as many treasures as they can manage.

To locate treasures, contestants may perform one of the following actions:
\begin{itemize}
	\renewcommand\labelitemi{\textbf{\textbullet}}
	\item \textbf{Spot Check (DC 20 Wisdom (Perception)):} Locate a treasure anywhere in the play area (DC 15 if searching within the \textbf{Inner Hoard}).
	\item \textbf{Search Check (DC 10 Intelligence (Investigation)):} Locate a treasure in the near vicinity. Searching in this way within the dragon's reach provokes an \textbf{Opportunity Attack} from the dragon.
\end{itemize}
If a contestant succeeds on their check, they can roll on the \textbf{Treasure Table} to determine the item found.

Contestants may continue to search and gather treasure for as long as they wish, carrying as many items as they can manage. However, treasures are forfeited if a contestant is struck by the dragon's attack. To remain in the spirit of the game, hit contestants are expected to leave the game when they are struck and may not interact with the hoard further, though they can remain in place as casualties for the remainder of the game.

At any time contestants may leave the treasure hoard, taking their gathered loot with them. Once a contestant leaves the playing area, however, they cannot re-enter, ending their participation in the game.}

\section*{Tree Game}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Tree Game}
\textbf{Entry Fee:} 1 GP\\
{\entryfont The Tree Game is a lively and challenging test of accuracy and skill, where contestants attempt to claim prizes nestled among the high branches of a towering tree. Using arrows, handaxes, or throwing knives, competitors aim for trinkets, baubles, and even valuable treasures carefully lodged within the tree's limbs. Each well-placed strike sends a prize tumbling gracefully to the ground, greeted by the crowd's cheers - or the envious gazes of onlookers hoping for their turn.}

\begin{DndReadAloud}
	Before you stands a majestic oak, its sprawling branches adorned with glittering prizes that catch the light like stars in the daylight. Trinkets dangle from ribbons and strings, while more valuable treasures are wedged tightly into crooks and knots high above. The lower branches sway gently with small, easy-to-reach prizes, while the loftiest rewards glint enticingly just out of easy range. Contestants gather at the base of the tree, readying their arrows, handaxes, or knives as the crowd murmurs in anticipation.

	\textit{"Step forward and take your shot!"} calls the event's master of ceremonies. \textit{"What will you claim from the tree's bounty - luck, skill, or sheer determination?"}

	The air hums with excitement as the first competitor lines up their throw, aiming to send fortune falling from the great tree's branches.
\end{DndReadAloud}

\subsection*{Tree Game - Mechanics}
{\entryfont In the Tree Game, contestants are tasked with dislodging prizes tied to the branches of the grand festival tree using borrowed weapons provided by the game organizers. Each participant may choose either a shortbow with 5 arrows or two handaxes, both of which must be returned at the end of the game. Over the course of five rounds, contestants attempt to target and knock down the dangling prizes.

Using the shortbow requires precise aim, as contestants must hit the harder AC listed in the Prize Table to sever the ribbons holding the prizes in place. Conversely, handaxes rely on brute force, allowing contestants to attack against the Standard AC of the target. Regardless of the weapon used, any prize that is successfully dislodged and falls to the ground is immediately claimed by the contestant.}

\begin{DndOptionalRule}{Losing Thrown Item}
	A Critical Failure (NAT 1) leads to the thrown item to be stuck in the tree as well, and needs to be retrieved/dislodged in a similar manner like the prizes. Otherwise it will be lost to the game.

\section*{Trollbridge}\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Trollbridge}
\textbf{Entry Fee:} 5 GP\\
{\entryfont The Trollbridge is a lively contest of balance, strength, and daring, where participants take on the role of nimble "Goats" attempting to cross a precarious series of raised platforms - all while a hulking "Troll" seeks to bar their way.}
\subsection*{Trollbridge - Mechanics}
{\entryfont In the Trollbridge, four contestants ("Goats") compete simultaneously, attempting to cross a series of three raised platforms (10-by-10 feet, 3 ft. apart), while a showmen - the "Troll" (Medium Size, Athletics +3, Insight +4) - occupies the central platform and seeks to hinder them. The Goats begin on the outer platforms.

\paragraph*{Turn-Based Mode} The Trollbridge Game uses standard combat timing. Each participant takes turns in Initiative Order, and on their turn may move and take an Action, as in a typical combat encounter.

\paragraph*{Gameplay} A Goat scores 1 point each time it successfully reaches the opposite platform. Movement between platforms requires a Jump and landing is unstable and treated as Difficult Terrain.

During each of its turns, the Troll uses the Ready Action to prepare one of the following:
\begin{itemize}
	\renewcommand\labelitemi{\textbf{\textbullet}}
	\item \textbf{Attack a Nearby Goat.}\\
	The Troll attacks with a sack of potatoes (-4 to hit, \DndDice{1d4} Bludgeoning damage, non-lethal). On a hit, the target must succeed on a Strength (Athletics) Check, DC 10 + damage taken, or be knocked Prone. If the check fails by 5 or more, the Goat falls from the platform.
	\item \textbf{Block a Jumping Goat.}\\
	The Troll moves to the space a Goat wants to jump to and both make a contested Strength (Athletics) Check. If the Goat wins, it forces its way through, pushing the Troll back to its original position. If the Troll wins the contest, the Goat must also succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) Check or fall from the platform. On a success, the Goat may instead drop Prone or attempt to leap back to its starting platform (DC 26 Strength (Athletics) Check).
\end{itemize}
Goats may attempt to deceive the troll when jumping by making a Deception Check contested by the Troll's Insight. On a success, the Troll commits to blocking the wrong space.

All participants may use the Shove action. Allied Goats may assist another Goat's shove attempt with a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) Check, granting a +2 bonus.

\paragraph*{Elimination} Any Goat that falls from a platform or is knocked Unconscious is removed from the game. A Troll that falls from the table is not eliminated but must spend its next turn climbing back onto the central platform.

\paragraph*{Goal} The Goats win collectively if they reach 24 points, whereas the Troll wins if all goats are eliminated before.}
\subsection*{Trollbridge - Rewards}
{\entryfont If the participants (Goats) win, they are each rewarded with the following prizes:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Bejewelled Drinking Horn (Goat) (40 GP)
	\item "Troll" Hide Armor (just a common Hide Armor)
\end{itemize}}
