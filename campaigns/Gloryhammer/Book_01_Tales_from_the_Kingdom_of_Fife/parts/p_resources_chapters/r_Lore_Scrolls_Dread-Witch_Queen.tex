\subsection*{Journal Fragments - Apprentice}
The scroll is bound with mismatched thread and stained with dried ink, ash, and faint traces of candle wax. Several entries appear written days or weeks apart.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

{\entryfont The boy from Crail returned again today.

Too curious for his own good. Too frightened to admit it.

He asks the right questions, though. Most who seek me crave power. He seeks impossibility.}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

{\entryfont He brought me another offering. Family trinkets this time. Cheap silver lockets containing painted portraits of his parents. Dead for nearly six winters now, if his memory serves him correctly.

He still believes death is a wall.

Adorable.}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

{\entryfont He wishes to preserve his sister from "the sickness that comes for the blood".

Mortals always phrase things poetically when they are desperate.

He asked if souls can be tethered before death.

The answer, of course, is yes.}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

{\entryfont Progress at last.

He no longer trembles during the rites. He learns quickly, though his moral hesitation remains tiresome. I showed him the preliminary soul-binding sigils and he understood their structure after only minor correction.

Had he been born two centuries earlier, he would have made a remarkable disciple.}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

{\entryfont A section further down is written entirely in flowing Elvish script, accompanied by ritual diagrams and concentric circles marked with lunar phases.}

{\elvishfont Ela thira vael nor en shal. Ithrakar vel shuun elessir. Thalanor ve mirath kae selun.}*

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

{\entryfont He departed for Crail several weeks ago.

No message. No raven. No failed experiment exploding in the distance.

Nothing.

Disappointing.

I had thought he might survive long enough to become useful.}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

\textit{*A character who succeeds on a DC 17 Intelligence Check can decipher the ritual's purpose. The scroll then functions as a Spell Scroll of Ceremony.}

\subsection*{Constructs shall Dream}
This scroll is densely packed with diagrams of construct anatomy, arcane circuitry, and fragmented notes written in obsessive detail.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

{\entryfont Flesh fears pain. Machines do not.

Thus the soul itself must be taught fear.

The earliest attempts proved ineffective. Lesser automatons simply ceased functioning, while advanced constructs resisted the intrusion entirely. Standard necromancy could not grasp minds built from logic and ordered enchantment.

The breakthrough came through dreams.

By inducing an artificial dream-state within the animating core of a construct, emotional echoes could be introduced directly into the soul matrix. Fear, panic, despair—all translated into recursive arcane feedback. Constructs exposed long enough began exhibiting hesitation, irrational movement patterns, and eventual collapse of core stability.

Nightmares are superior to blades. A sword destroys the body. Terror destroys purpose.}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

The final notes describe the ritual as complete and fully weaponized against the constructs of Auchtertool.
\subsection*{Swamps of Paisley}
This scroll is written in a noticeably more casual tone than the others, almost irritated.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

{\entryfont Rumors persist regarding some ancient evil hidden deep within the Swamps of Paisley. Local villagers speak of vanished travelers, whispering lights, and "something beneath the water". Predictably superstitious nonsense.

I ventured into the swamps personally on three separate occasions expecting at least some worthwhile discovery. Instead, I found mud, insects, foul-smelling water, and elderly fishermen eager to invent increasingly ridiculous stories to frighten children away from wandering too far from home.

One claimed an entire island disappears when viewed directly.

Another insisted the swamp itself "breathes".

The oldest swore something watches from beneath the waterline during the new moon.

Utter folklore.

If there truly is power hidden within those swamps, it is doing an exceptional job of remaining irrelevant.}

\subsection*{A Letter Never Sent}
Unlike the others, this parchment appears unfinished. Several lines are violently scratched out before continuing.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

{\entryfont To the Lordship of Auchtertool,

You worship imitation life while fearing true transcendence.

Your machines walk, speak, obey, and yet you still insist they are tools rather than heirs. You stand upon the edge of revelation and refuse to look downward.

The Mecha-Lord understands.

I know he does.

He hears the same silence beneath the world that I do.

You replace failing flesh with steel because some part of you already knows the truth: mortality is an engineering flaw.

When Ravenscraig is secured, I will offer him alliance one final time.

If he refuses again-}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

The remainder of the scroll has been burned away intentionally.
\subsection*{Dreams Beneath the Waves}
This scroll is old - far older than the others - and portions of the parchment feel strangely damp despite being stored indoors.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}

{\entryfont There are references in forgotten coastal records to something sleeping beneath the Forth of Fife, deep below the cliffs and black waters near the eastern coast. Not a beast. Not a god.

A dream.

Ancient sailors described hearing voices beneath the tides during storms, promising knowledge in exchange for memory. Entire crews vanished after claiming they had seen "golden eyes beneath the waves".

The records become increasingly fragmented afterwards. Most end abruptly or are overwritten by religious authorities.}

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.6pt}\\
One line remains legible near the bottom of the scroll:
\begin{center}
	\entryfont "Should the Sleeper awaken, even death itself shall dream."
\end{center}
